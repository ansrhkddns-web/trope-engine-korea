# Variation Engine

Use this file when a cliche is correct but feels too familiar. The goal is not to hide the cliche. The goal is to preserve the expected reward and change the path to that reward.

## Familiar-but-Fresh Formula

1. Name the familiar core:
   - "weak awakener rank test reversal"
   - "regressor buys future asset"
   - "hidden dungeon condition"
   - "sect disciple duel upset"
   - "chaebol boardroom revenge"

2. Name the reader reward:
   - status reversal
   - money
   - power
   - revenge
   - social recognition
   - knowledge superiority
   - safety/rescue
   - relationship shift

3. Change one or two variation axes:
   - **Cost**: what the win demands.
   - **Institution**: who measures, certifies, blocks, or exploits the win.
   - **Proof object**: what visible thing proves value.
   - **Witness**: who sees it and why that matters.
   - **Arena**: where the cliche happens.
   - **Moral frame**: whether the act looks heroic, illegal, demonic, selfish, or suspicious.
   - **Timing**: whether the payoff comes before, during, or after the expected scene.

4. Add a consequence:
   - new enemy
   - debt
   - contract
   - public rumor
   - investigation
   - family reaction
   - sponsor interest
   - market distortion
   - future event changing

5. Make the variation scene-visible:
   - Do not only rename the system.
   - Show a changed test, auction, meeting, gate, duel, contract, broadcast, or public reaction.

## Variation Axes

### Cost

Use cost when the protagonist is becoming too effortless.

- A hidden title requires public responsibility.
- A future stock purchase creates insider-trading suspicion.
- A gate exploit saves civilians but ruins a guild's raid profit.
- A martial manual cures meridians but marks the protagonist as demonic.
- A sponsor contract gives power but restricts future choices.

### Institution

Use institution when the world feels thin.

- Hunter association as insurer, labor regulator, disaster agency, sports league, or licensing board.
- Tower administrators as contract judges, algorithm moderators, or divine bureaucracy.
- Chaebol family as court, cult, investment committee, or intelligence network.
- Martial sect as school, hospital, bank, military, or religious order.
- Academy as talent market, media show, guild draft, or noble marriage market.

### Proof Object

Use proof object when rewards feel abstract.

- status window line
- ranking board update
- auction bid
- rescue footage
- share certificate
- land deed
- guild contract
- sect token
- duel scar
- medicine effect
- rumor headline
- sponsor message

### Witness

Use witness when a scene lacks social pleasure.

- the enemy who dismissed the protagonist
- a future ally before they are famous
- a parent, elder, professor, auditor, reporter, prosecutor, guild scout, or sponsor
- ordinary civilians whose reaction proves moral value
- an institution that now owes the protagonist something

### Arena

Use arena when the trope is too predictable.

- rank test during live audit
- dungeon break during board meeting
- sect duel inside a medicine hall
- land auction tied to future gate risk
- tower tutorial treated as contract negotiation
- academy exam sponsored by guild IPO investors
- demonic cult succession trial run like a corporate takeover

## Combination Recipes

### Hunter + Finance

- Gates produce resources with futures markets.
- Guilds are companies with raid rights, insurance, and IPO value.
- A low-rank hunter may be rich because they control land, patents, logistics, or monster byproducts.
- Good first proof: protagonist buys ignored gate-adjacent land, then uses a dungeon break to prove both combat and business insight.

### Regression + Chaebol

- Future knowledge must pass through capital, legal proof, and family suspicion.
- Make early wins small but decisive: one subsidiary, one land deed, one patent, one board vote.
- Add suspicion: how did the protagonist know?
- Good first proof: saves a doomed subsidiary with a move that also humiliates the relative who framed them.

### Tower + Constellation + Streaming

- Sponsors are not only gods; they are patrons, investors, fandoms, political blocs, or algorithms.
- Rewards become public performance: coins, contracts, comments, votes, reputation.
- Good first proof: protagonist refuses a flashy sponsor and accepts a low-status contract with a hidden clause.

### Martial Arts + System

- The system measures martial growth, but old institutions decide legitimacy.
- Conflict comes from mismatch between measured power and accepted honor.
- Good first proof: system says the protagonist is weak, but a sect elder recognizes a forbidden precision the device cannot value.

### Possession + Academy

- The protagonist knows plot events, but social scripts still pressure them.
- Avoid simply stealing all opportunities. Make each stolen opportunity alter relationships.
- Good first proof: protagonist avoids the expected villain scene, creating confusion and a new enemy who depended on that script.

### Martial Arts + Finance

- Sects own mines, medicine routes, debt contracts, manuals, and protection rights.
- Realm breakthroughs have economic impact: pill monopolies, escort contracts, martial labor.
- Good first proof: protagonist wins a duel by controlling medicine supply and honor debt, not only technique.

## Anti-Staleness Checks

Mark a trope stale if:

- the protagonist wins because the author says so, not because of a visible method;
- the rank/status number changes without social consequence;
- the same humiliation/reversal repeats with no new type of witness;
- future knowledge has no cost, uncertainty, or changed timeline;
- the institution exists only to be stupid;
- rewards are delayed for too long without small payoffs;
- the fresh element is just new terminology;
- the secondary genre blocks the primary genre promise.

## Repair Moves

- Move the first proof earlier.
- Add one witness who matters.
- Turn explanation into a test, bid, duel, audit, gate, contract, or broadcast.
- Add a cost after the reward, not before every reward.
- Make the institution rational but misaligned.
- Replace generic villain anger with a concrete loss: money, rank, face, vote, disciple, contract, land, sponsor, or evidence.
- Tie each new setting rule to a future reward or threat.
