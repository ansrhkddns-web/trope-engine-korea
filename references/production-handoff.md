# Production Handoff

Use this file when the user wants to move from trope design into a project bible, episode roadmap, episode brief, manuscript drafting packet, or revision work order.

## Handoff Principle

A trope engine is not finished until it tells the next worker what to write, prove, withhold, and avoid. Turn abstract planning into production instructions.

## Artifact Ladder

| User needs | Produce | Include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rough idea | quick trope pack | promise, advantage, first proof, 5 cliche cards |
| reusable concept | trope engine spec | schema fields, reward ledger, package direction |
| story bible | bible insert | world engine, institutions, cards, escalation, continuity rules |
| early serialization | 1-25 or 1-50 roadmap | episode functions, rewards, hooks, institution reactions |
| one episode | episode production brief | scene order, proof, reward, withheld answer, ending hook |
| prose drafting | manuscript drafting packet | scene purpose, conflict, evidence, exit pressure |
| revision | repair work order | weakest axis, exact replacement scene, banned mistakes |

## Handoff Packet

Include:

- **Current artifact**: what has been designed.
- **Next artifact**: what should be produced next.
- **Non-negotiable promise**: the reader reward that must not disappear.
- **First proof scene**: the scene that proves the engine.
- **Reward ledger**: what has been promised and what must be paid.
- **Approved cliche cards**: reusable devices.
- **Restricted cliche cards**: stale, risky, or off-promise devices.
- **Continuity anchors**: names, institutions, rules, debts, contracts, ranks, assets.
- **Do not write**: scenes, explanations, or tropes that would weaken the concept.
- **Next 3 tasks**: concrete next actions.

## Episode Brief Bridge

When turning a trope engine into an episode brief, include:

- episode number and function;
- previous hook response;
- opening pressure;
- protagonist decision;
- trope card used;
- proof object;
- visible reward;
- witness reaction;
- withheld answer;
- ending hook object;
- next-click question;
- must include;
- must avoid.

## Bible Bridge

When turning a trope engine into a project bible, include:

- genre contract;
- world engine;
- institution map;
- protagonist engine;
- ally/rival/enemy functions;
- reward ledger rules;
- approved trope cards;
- banned trope patterns;
- 1-50 escalation;
- product package;
- originality safety notes;
- continuity tracking fields.

## Revision Work Order

When diagnosing an existing plan, end with a work order:

1. Move or rewrite the first proof scene.
2. Add or change the proof object.
3. Add the witness who must react.
4. Remove the stale or off-promise trope.
5. Add the cost after the reward.
6. Update title/logline if the promise changed.
7. Update the next 3 episode functions.

## Integration With Other Writing Skills

When the user asks for:

- full Korean commercial project design, pair with `k-webnovel-architect`;
- episode hooks, cliffhangers, paid conversion, or episode-level diagnostics, pair with `episode-hook-designer`;
- prose drafting or scene writing, pair with `scene-construction` or `prose-writing`;
- chapter briefs or chapter drafting, pair with `chapter-writing`;
- character or worldbuilding expansions, pair with `character-management` or `worldbuilding`.

Keep this skill responsible for trope engine, reward logic, proof scenes, packaging, and freshness. Let the other skill own its specialized output.
