# Reader Response Simulation

Use this file to predict how readers may react to a trope pack, first proof scene, title package, or early episode plan.

## Simulation Modes

- **quick pulse**: likely praise, likely complaint, likely comments.
- **drop-off scan**: where readers may stop clicking.
- **comment bait design**: add a debate, theory, anger, relief, or anticipation point.
- **paid trust scan**: whether a paid boundary feels fair.
- **genre promise scan**: whether readers who clicked the title get the expected pleasure.

## Reaction Channels

| Channel | Positive reaction | Negative reaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | "This is the genre fun I wanted." | "What genre is this supposed to be?" |
| Competence | "The protagonist is clever." | "The protagonist is just lucky." |
| Catharsis | "Finally, that person got punished." | "The humiliation is dragging." |
| Reward | "The win was satisfying." | "Nothing actually changed." |
| Freshness | "I know this trope, but the route is new." | "Only the terms changed." |
| Trust | "The hook makes me want the next answer." | "This is just bait." |
| Social proof | "I liked seeing everyone react." | "No one important noticed." |

## Comment Trigger Types

- **theory**: "Why did the system classify him differently?"
- **anger**: "That guild/family/sect needs to be punished."
- **choice debate**: "Should he accept the contract or refuse?"
- **shipping/relationship**: "This ally noticed him first."
- **competence praise**: "He won without brute force."
- **reward anticipation**: "Show the auction/duel/ranking reaction next."
- **moral debate**: "Was it right to save the future villain?"

## Drop-Off Risks

Mark risk if:

- the first proof is delayed past episode 1;
- the protagonist's method is unclear;
- the reward is private and no one reacts;
- the same humiliation repeats without new payoff;
- the title promises money/revenge/rank but the episode delivers only setup;
- the hook withholds the first reward instead of opening a new pressure;
- readers need too much lore to understand why the scene matters.

## Repair Moves

- Add one proof object.
- Add one witness with power.
- Pay one small promise before opening a larger mystery.
- Convert a vague hook into a concrete object, message, name, contract, ranking, bid, or duel.
- Add a comment-worthy choice before the ending.
- Make the next-click question one sentence.
