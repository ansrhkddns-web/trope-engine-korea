# Cliche Structure Assembly Rules

Use this file after loading genre survey values and genre-specific 100-item cliche banks. It explains how to turn large cliche lists into usable engines instead of dumping ideas.

## Core Principle

A cliche becomes useful only when it has a job in the serial engine.

Structure every selected cliche with:

- **role**: identity, advantage, proof, reaction, renewal, long-term, emotion, economy, mystery, or antagonist.
- **reward**: power, money, status, revenge, knowledge, safety, relationship, recognition, or autonomy.
- **proof**: visible object, measurable result, public witness, institution reaction, or irreversible choice.
- **cost**: debt, enemy, attention, lost option, moral pressure, time limit, legal/faction risk.
- **variation**: changed witness, institution, proof object, timing, method, cost, or genre collision.

## 10-Card Trope Engine

For a complete concept, choose exactly one or two from each slot:

| Slot | Job | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Opening identity | tells readers what kind of protagonist they are following | F-rank, doomed villain, fallen heir, outer disciple |
| 2. Unfair advantage | creates action others cannot take | regression memory, system loophole, appraisal, hidden manual |
| 3. First pressure | forces a choice now | exam, gate break, board vote, duel, accusation, contract |
| 4. Visible proof | proves the fun in scene | rank board, rescued person, auction price, public apology |
| 5. Social amplifier | makes the win feel bigger | scout, rival, elder, media, family head, professor |
| 6. Immediate cost | prevents free victory | debt, ownership claim, penalty, suspicion, changed timeline |
| 7. Repeatable loop | produces episodes | test -> proof -> reaction -> access -> stronger test |
| 8. Antagonist mirror | keeps the cliche contested | second regressor, rival system, hostile investor, false hero |
| 9. Long-term resource | lets the story scale | guild, company, sect, territory, sponsor network, monopoly |
| 10. Package hook | sells the promise | title noun, rank, contract, title, named enemy, countdown |

## 6-Role Success Stack

When the user asks for widely used or successful cliches, assemble:

1. **Identity cliche**: who the protagonist is at the start.
2. **Advantage cliche**: what lets the protagonist act.
3. **Proof cliche**: how the advantage becomes visible.
4. **Social reaction cliche**: who recognizes, envies, fears, or denies the proof.
5. **Renewal cliche**: what new problem the proof creates.
6. **Long-term cliche**: what keeps the engine alive after the first arc.

Do not use more than two cliches with the same role unless diagnosing a stale concept.

## 5-Beat Episode Loop

Use this for episode seeds:

1. **Pressure**: a test, gate, duel, rumor, deadline, vote, route event, or contract.
2. **Exploit**: the protagonist uses the advantage.
3. **Proof**: the result is visible.
4. **Reaction**: an institution or witness changes behavior.
5. **Renewal**: the reward opens a new cost, enemy, debt, clue, or offer.

The renewal must be connected to the proof. Random cliffhangers reduce trust.

## Cross-Genre Combination Rules

- **Primary genre owns the first proof**: if it is hunter, prove gate value first; if romance fantasy, prove relationship/status shift first.
- **Support genre supplies cost or scale**: finance can turn hunter loot into market pressure; academy can turn tower skills into public ranking.
- **Delayed flavor waits until the reader trusts the core**: do not introduce five genre engines in episode 1.
- **Use one shared proof object**: a contract, rank board, deed, manual, or recording can connect genres better than explanation.
- **Never stack terms only**: "hunter + chaebol + system + academy" must become "gate rights are school-sponsored assets measured by a public rank board."

## Cliche Diversity Rules

When selecting from a 100-item genre bank:

- Pick 3 familiar cliches readers instantly understand.
- Pick 2 structural cliches that create repeatable scenes.
- Pick 1 social reaction cliche.
- Pick 1 cost cliche.
- Pick 1 long-term antagonist or mystery cliche.
- Pick 1 cliche from another genre as a controlled collision.
- Pick 1 freshness lever that changes scene mechanics.

## Anti-Staleness Checks

Mark the plan stale if:

- the protagonist's advantage produces no choice;
- all wins are private;
- all rewards are numbers;
- every enemy is foolish;
- the same proof scene repeats with higher stats only;
- the social institution never changes behavior;
- the opening promise is paid only after many chapters;
- the "freshness" is only a renamed class, sect, company, or kingdom.

Repair by changing proof object, witness, institution, cost, or timing.
