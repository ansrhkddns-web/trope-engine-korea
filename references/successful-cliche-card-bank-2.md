# Successful Cliche Card Bank 2

Use this card bank when the user wants widely used, commercially proven webnovel cliches. These cards focus on common success patterns in Korean, Chinese, Japanese, and global progression-style web fiction, translated into Korean commercial webnovel use.

## Progression and Power Cards

### Slow Growth That Proves Method

- **Function**: make growth feel earned and repeatable.
- **Basic form**: protagonist starts weak but has a method only they understand.
- **Reward**: competence, anticipation, measurable rise.
- **Proof scene**: the same method works twice under different pressure.
- **Fresh variation**: the method is support, logistics, negotiation, rescue, or information rather than training alone.
- **Use when**: the story risks becoming too overpowered too quickly.

### Hidden Class Evolution

- **Function**: give a long-term identity ladder.
- **Basic form**: weak class evolves into rare class after hidden conditions.
- **Reward**: exclusivity, power, speculation.
- **Proof scene**: class evolves publicly or causes an institution to reclassify the protagonist.
- **Fresh variation**: class evolution creates liability, tax, sponsor claim, or taboo.
- **Use when**: the concept needs repeated milestone rewards.

### Skill Theft With Ethical Cost

- **Function**: give fast power while creating moral pressure.
- **Basic form**: protagonist copies, steals, absorbs, or inherits skills.
- **Reward**: power, strategy, collection pleasure.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist uses a copied skill in a way the original owner could not.
- **Fresh variation**: stealing skills creates memories, debts, side effects, or public suspicion.
- **Use when**: the protagonist needs flexible tactics but must not feel free.

### Status Window Lie

- **Function**: make system information unreliable or incomplete.
- **Basic form**: status window says one thing, reality proves another.
- **Reward**: mystery, intelligence, fresh system use.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist beats a status prediction through hidden variable.
- **Fresh variation**: the window measures institutional value, not true potential.
- **Use when**: the system feels too convenient.

## Regression and Knowledge Cards

### Future Event Deadline

- **Function**: give regression a clock.
- **Basic form**: protagonist knows an event will happen on a specific date.
- **Reward**: suspense, preparation payoff.
- **Proof scene**: one preparation works, but the event changes because of intervention.
- **Fresh variation**: saving one person accelerates another threat.
- **Use when**: regression scenes lack urgency.

### Memory Gap

- **Function**: prevent future knowledge from solving everything.
- **Basic form**: protagonist remembers major events but not exact causes or hidden actors.
- **Reward**: uncertainty, investigation, earned intelligence.
- **Proof scene**: remembered outcome is right, but the mechanism is different.
- **Fresh variation**: gaps cluster around a person, institution, or erased timeline.
- **Use when**: future knowledge is too strong.

### Failed Future Ally

- **Function**: make recruitment emotionally and strategically interesting.
- **Basic form**: future hero is currently broken, disgraced, poor, addicted, cursed, or hated.
- **Reward**: relationship, anticipation, rescue.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist helps the ally show one glimpse of future greatness.
- **Fresh variation**: ally refuses to be "collected" and demands a bargain.
- **Use when**: team building feels like trophy collection.

### Second Knower

- **Function**: make the protagonist's meta advantage contested.
- **Basic form**: another regressor, possessor, returnee, prophet, or system-favored person exists.
- **Reward**: suspense, strategic rivalry.
- **Proof scene**: the protagonist sees an event changed before they acted.
- **Fresh variation**: second knower has different incomplete information.
- **Use when**: the story needs a long-term equal.

## Social Reversal and Face Cards

### Competent Rival Face-Slap

- **Function**: make public reversal satisfying without making the world dumb.
- **Basic form**: rival is genuinely good but misreads the protagonist's value.
- **Reward**: recognition, competitive thrill.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist wins by solving a different problem than the rival optimized for.
- **Fresh variation**: rival loses face but gains respect and adapts.
- **Use when**: face-slapping risks feeling childish.

### Crowd Turns in Stages

- **Function**: make recognition feel earned.
- **Basic form**: crowd goes from mockery to doubt to silence to reaction.
- **Reward**: public proof, catharsis.
- **Proof scene**: each stage is triggered by a visible clue.
- **Fresh variation**: only one sharp observer recognizes the truth early.
- **Use when**: public reaction feels abrupt.

### Scout Bidding War

- **Function**: convert proof into market/social value.
- **Basic form**: guilds, companies, sects, sponsors, or professors compete for protagonist.
- **Reward**: status, leverage, choice.
- **Proof scene**: multiple offers arrive after a public result.
- **Fresh variation**: best offer is not highest power but best constraint.
- **Use when**: the story needs social proof after a win.

### Public Apology Trap

- **Function**: turn revenge into a public procedure.
- **Basic form**: enemy must apologize, retract, compensate, or lose authority.
- **Reward**: catharsis, justice.
- **Proof scene**: apology happens in the same venue as humiliation.
- **Fresh variation**: apology is legally useful but socially dangerous.
- **Use when**: revenge needs a smaller early payoff.

## Institution and Economy Cards

### Wrong KPI Institution

- **Function**: make institutions rational but flawed.
- **Basic form**: association/school/sect/company measures the wrong success metric.
- **Reward**: intelligence, social critique, fresh proof.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist's alternate metric predicts survival, profit, or loyalty better.
- **Fresh variation**: changing the metric threatens someone's business model.
- **Use when**: institutions look stupid.

### Resource Monopoly Seed

- **Function**: turn early knowledge into long-term power.
- **Basic form**: protagonist controls an undervalued resource before demand appears.
- **Reward**: money, leverage, strategic growth.
- **Proof scene**: first public demand spike proves the resource.
- **Fresh variation**: monopoly creates regulation, ethical pressure, or sabotage.
- **Use when**: finance/territory/gate concepts need a long engine.

### Auction Reveal

- **Function**: make market recognition immediate.
- **Basic form**: protagonist buys or sells an item others misvalue.
- **Reward**: money, competence, public reaction.
- **Proof scene**: a second bidder or later appraisal reveals true value.
- **Fresh variation**: the item is evidence, debt, key, or legal claim, not only power.
- **Use when**: the story needs a fast public proof without combat.

### Contract Clause Power-Up

- **Function**: make rules into weapons.
- **Basic form**: a contract, oath, system clause, or inheritance rule gives leverage.
- **Reward**: intelligence, autonomy.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist turns the clause against the drafter.
- **Fresh variation**: the clause gives victory but binds the protagonist to a future duty.
- **Use when**: the story needs cleverness over raw power.

## Academy, Sect, and Training Cards

### Entrance Exam Category Error

- **Function**: prove the protagonist is strong in a category the school does not respect.
- **Basic form**: exam ranks combat highest; protagonist wins by support, tactics, rescue, or rule reading.
- **Reward**: underdog proof, social reaction.
- **Proof scene**: examiner must create a new category or exception.
- **Fresh variation**: the exception makes the protagonist politically inconvenient.
- **Use when**: academy opening needs freshness.

### Teacher Notices the Wrong Thing

- **Function**: add early elite recognition without full public proof.
- **Basic form**: a teacher/elder/professor notices a small abnormal choice.
- **Reward**: anticipation, mentorship, hidden value.
- **Proof scene**: the mentor's private note contradicts public score.
- **Fresh variation**: mentor is not benevolent; they want to use the protagonist.
- **Use when**: the story needs a slow-burn recognition path.

### Training Method Taboo

- **Function**: give growth a social cost.
- **Basic form**: protagonist uses a forbidden, low-status, demonic, outdated, or humiliating training method.
- **Reward**: power, identity, tension.
- **Proof scene**: method works publicly but triggers taboo response.
- **Fresh variation**: taboo exists for a valid historical reason.
- **Use when**: training scenes need conflict.

### Tournament With Hidden Scoring

- **Function**: prevent tournaments from becoming only fight ladders.
- **Basic form**: scoring includes teamwork, rescue, resource use, contract goals, or audience trust.
- **Reward**: strategy, team dynamics, public proof.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist loses visible fight but wins hidden score, or vice versa.
- **Fresh variation**: hidden scoring was designed by an enemy but backfires.
- **Use when**: tournament arc risks predictability.

## Relationship and Emotional Cards

### Rescue First, Loyalty Later

- **Function**: make loyalty earned.
- **Basic form**: protagonist saves someone without immediate reward.
- **Reward**: emotional trust, future payoff.
- **Proof scene**: rescued person later provides decisive witness or access.
- **Fresh variation**: rescued person initially hates or fears protagonist.
- **Use when**: allies feel too easy.

### Enemy Child / Future Villain Rescue

- **Function**: create moral choice and future suspense.
- **Basic form**: protagonist can save someone who becomes dangerous later.
- **Reward**: moral tension, relationship complexity.
- **Proof scene**: saving them changes one future event.
- **Fresh variation**: rescue creates suspicion among current allies.
- **Use when**: regression/possession needs emotional depth.

### Misunderstood Kindness

- **Function**: give emotional payoff without weakening competence.
- **Basic form**: protagonist's harsh or suspicious action protects someone.
- **Reward**: delayed recognition, relationship shift.
- **Proof scene**: evidence reveals the hidden protective purpose.
- **Fresh variation**: only one character learns the truth, creating private loyalty.
- **Use when**: character appeal needs warmth.

## Hook and Serialization Cards

### Reward-Then-Cost Ending

- **Function**: keep trust while creating next-click pressure.
- **Basic form**: episode pays reward, then reveals price.
- **Reward**: satisfaction plus tension.
- **Proof scene**: rank up, contract accepted, item acquired, then owner/enemy/timer appears.
- **Fresh variation**: cost comes from institution reaction, not random punishment.
- **Use when**: cliffhangers feel like bait.

### Named Threat Appears

- **Function**: make the next click concrete.
- **Basic form**: a name, title, guild, elder, sponsor, buyer, or family head appears.
- **Reward**: anticipation, scale.
- **Proof scene**: protagonist's win triggers attention from a higher layer.
- **Fresh variation**: named threat is not immediately hostile but wants control.
- **Use when**: escalation needs a clean handoff.

### Comment Debate Choice

- **Function**: create reader engagement.
- **Basic form**: protagonist must accept/refuse, reveal/hide, save/abandon, expose/wait.
- **Reward**: agency, comment fuel.
- **Proof scene**: both options have clear reward and cost.
- **Fresh variation**: readers can argue because both choices are rational.
- **Use when**: episode endings lack discussion hooks.
