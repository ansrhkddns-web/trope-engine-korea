# Cliche Remix Operators

Use this file when a trope pack has enough cliches but still feels predictable. Operators change how a familiar cliche is proven, witnessed, priced, or repeated without destroying the expected reader reward.

Do not apply more than two strong operators to one cliche at the same time. If everything is twisted, the genre promise becomes hard to recognize.

## Operator Schema

Each operator should define:

- **target**: what part of the cliche it changes.
- **keeps**: the familiar reward that must remain.
- **changes**: the visible scene mechanics.
- **best genres**: where it tends to work.
- **failure risk**: how it can break the story.

## Core Remix Operators

### O01 Metric Swap

- **target**: proof object.
- **keeps**: public recognition.
- **changes**: measure combat as survival, profit, liability, rescue, trust, legality, or social value.
- **best genres**: hunter, academy, tower, finance, support class.
- **failure risk**: metric feels invented unless planted before the payoff.

### O02 Witness Swap

- **target**: social proof.
- **keeps**: satisfaction of recognition.
- **changes**: replace crowd reaction with rival, auditor, victim, market, elder, family retainer, professor, system, or media.
- **best genres**: all.
- **failure risk**: witness has no authority or emotional weight.

### O03 Cost Injection

- **target**: renewal.
- **keeps**: reward.
- **changes**: every gain creates debt, owner, lawsuit, taboo, forced offer, tax, public suspicion, or future uncertainty.
- **best genres**: regression, system, finance, romance fantasy, murim.
- **failure risk**: punishment feels random if not caused by the reward.

### O04 Institution Transplant

- **target**: genre surface.
- **keeps**: cliche function.
- **changes**: move a cliche into another institution: sect trial -> board vote, rank test -> social debut, auction -> guild draft, marriage contract -> sponsor contract.
- **best genres**: cross-genre concepts.
- **failure risk**: transplanted institution does not actually measure the reward.

### O05 Ownership Reveal

- **target**: motif object.
- **keeps**: acquisition pleasure.
- **changes**: the item, title, land, skill, contract, or manual has a prior owner, hidden claimant, debt, or inherited obligation.
- **best genres**: tower, martial arts, finance, romance fantasy, hunter item market.
- **failure risk**: ownership is trivia with no consequence.

### O06 Competent Opposition

- **target**: antagonist quality.
- **keeps**: face-slap or reversal pleasure.
- **changes**: enemy is right by the old metric but loses to the protagonist's new metric.
- **best genres**: academy, guild, sect, boardroom, media.
- **failure risk**: opposition becomes too reasonable and the revenge payoff weakens.

### O07 Partial Reveal

- **target**: information timing.
- **keeps**: mystery and reader trust.
- **changes**: show readers enough proof to trust the protagonist while characters still misunderstand.
- **best genres**: regression, possession, misunderstood protector, revenge.
- **failure risk**: too much withheld information feels like manipulation.

### O08 Anti-Free-Power Constraint

- **target**: advantage.
- **keeps**: specialness.
- **changes**: add cooldown, proof burden, legal risk, moral cost, owner claim, social stigma, missing memory, or incompatible witness.
- **best genres**: system, regression, hidden class, skill theft.
- **failure risk**: constraint removes the fun if it blocks every reward.

### O09 Public Procedure

- **target**: revenge/payoff venue.
- **keeps**: catharsis.
- **changes**: make punishment happen through audit, hearing, board vote, duel law, school discipline, court, guild license review, or social ceremony.
- **best genres**: revenge, chaebol, academy, sect, romance fantasy.
- **failure risk**: procedure becomes slow bureaucracy without emotional stakes.

### O10 Role Inversion

- **target**: expected role.
- **keeps**: recognition signal.
- **changes**: donor needs help, false hero is structurally useful, villain child is rescue target, support class controls combat, rival becomes witness.
- **best genres**: possession, regression, academy, hunter, romance fantasy.
- **failure risk**: inversion erases why the original cliche was attractive.

### O11 Category Error

- **target**: classification.
- **keeps**: underdog payoff.
- **changes**: protagonist is not weak; they are measured under the wrong category.
- **best genres**: hunter rank, academy exam, hidden class, finance valuation, sect realm.
- **failure risk**: repeated wrong measurement makes institutions look foolish.

### O12 Delayed Owner

- **target**: hook.
- **keeps**: reward-then-cost click.
- **changes**: owner, sponsor, family, sect, patent holder, previous regressor, or system administrator appears after the reward.
- **best genres**: auction, hidden manual, title, deed, gate core, marriage contract.
- **failure risk**: owner appears from nowhere with no foreshadowing.

### O13 Moral Repricing

- **target**: reward value.
- **keeps**: strategic win.
- **changes**: a profitable or powerful choice costs trust, rescue chance, future ally, or innocent collateral.
- **best genres**: regression, finance, revenge, tower, apocalypse.
- **failure risk**: moral dilemma stalls action.

### O14 Support Function Preservation

- **target**: support-class growth.
- **keeps**: undervalued-role payoff.
- **changes**: protagonist grows by making support function more powerful, not by abandoning it for generic combat.
- **best genres**: porter, crafter, healer, logistics, medicine, manager, cooking.
- **failure risk**: support scenes become recipe lists without conflict.

### O15 Antagonist Mirror

- **target**: long-term conflict.
- **keeps**: protagonist advantage.
- **changes**: antagonist has a rival version: second regressor, hostile system, copied skill, alternate prophecy, investor using same future.
- **best genres**: regression, tower, system, finance, academy.
- **failure risk**: mirror invalidates the protagonist's promise if introduced too late.

### O16 Consequence Ladder

- **target**: escalation.
- **keeps**: repeatable reward.
- **changes**: personal win triggers team, institution, market, region, nation, and world consequences.
- **best genres**: long-running serials.
- **failure risk**: scale grows without preserving the original reward.

### O17 Proof Object Mutation

- **target**: motif.
- **keeps**: visible proof.
- **changes**: object changes category: item -> evidence, manual -> contract, title -> liability, deed -> gate key, apology -> legal admission.
- **best genres**: all.
- **failure risk**: mutation feels arbitrary unless the object had ambiguous signs.

### O18 Social Debt

- **target**: relationship reward.
- **keeps**: loyalty or recognition.
- **changes**: rescue, help, or kindness creates a future obligation, suspicion, dependency, or faction alignment.
- **best genres**: regression recruitment, romance fantasy, academy team, hunter rescue, murim debt.
- **failure risk**: allies feel transactional only.

### O19 Reputation Lag

- **target**: public reaction timing.
- **keeps**: eventual recognition.
- **changes**: proof is real but reputation changes slowly through rumors, reports, witnesses, market prices, and repeated small proof.
- **best genres**: romance fantasy, academy, support class, finance.
- **failure risk**: reader waits too long without payoff.

### O20 Genre Surface Split

- **target**: genre blend.
- **keeps**: primary promise.
- **changes**: first proof is one genre, cost is another: hunter proof creates finance cost, romance proof creates legal cost, murim duel creates media cost.
- **best genres**: hybrid concepts.
- **failure risk**: support genre hijacks the first click.

### O21 Timer Compression

- **target**: pacing.
- **keeps**: urgency.
- **changes**: compress a familiar cliche into a timer: gate break, board vote, poison spread, auction close, death flag date, floor penalty.
- **best genres**: all action/revenge engines.
- **failure risk**: every scene becomes breathless without payoff.

### O22 Venue Echo

- **target**: payoff location.
- **keeps**: catharsis.
- **changes**: payoff happens in the same venue where humiliation, accusation, rejection, or underestimation occurred.
- **best genres**: face-slap, revenge, academy, romance fantasy, chaebol.
- **failure risk**: too neat if the venue has no new pressure.

### O23 Hidden Audience

- **target**: witness.
- **keeps**: social proof.
- **changes**: a sponsor, elder, professor, investor, media account, original protagonist, or system admin is watching unseen.
- **best genres**: tower, academy, finance, murim, romance fantasy.
- **failure risk**: hidden audience becomes a convenient reward dispenser.

### O24 Rule Owner Retaliation

- **target**: taboo/rule exploit.
- **keeps**: clever exploit.
- **changes**: the owner of the rule reacts: association, system, sect, family, board, school, contract drafter.
- **best genres**: system, tower, guild, chaebol, sect.
- **failure risk**: retaliation is too strong and stops the premise.

### O25 Small Payoff First

- **target**: reader trust.
- **keeps**: long-term mystery.
- **changes**: pay one small concrete reward before opening a larger question.
- **best genres**: all serials.
- **failure risk**: small payoff is too small to offset confusion.

### O26 Motif Relay

- **target**: serial object.
- **keeps**: continuity.
- **changes**: one object moves through owners and meanings across arcs.
- **best genres**: manual, deed, contract, title, family heirloom, rank card, gate key.
- **failure risk**: object becomes lore baggage.

### O27 Comment-Debate Choice

- **target**: episode ending.
- **keeps**: agency.
- **changes**: end with two rational options: reveal/hide, save/abandon, accept/refuse, expose/wait, profit/protect.
- **best genres**: regression, revenge, romance fantasy, finance, academy.
- **failure risk**: false choice if one option is obviously correct.

### O28 Sympathy Reversal

- **target**: character relation.
- **keeps**: emotional payoff.
- **changes**: an enemy's reason becomes understandable while their method remains unacceptable.
- **best genres**: romance fantasy, academy, revenge, murim, regression.
- **failure risk**: softens the antagonist before payoff.

### O29 Public Metric Fork

- **target**: institution reaction.
- **keeps**: status rise.
- **changes**: public metric and expert metric disagree, creating two audiences.
- **best genres**: academy, hunter, finance, entertainment, romance fantasy.
- **failure risk**: readers do not know which metric matters.

### O30 Future Drift

- **target**: regression reliability.
- **keeps**: future knowledge pleasure.
- **changes**: every successful intervention changes a future clue, making knowledge useful but unstable.
- **best genres**: regression, possession, time loop.
- **failure risk**: drift turns into randomness.

## Operator Selection Presets

| Problem | Use operators |
| --- | --- |
| Too familiar | O01, O02, O05, O17 |
| Too easy | O03, O08, O12, O24 |
| Dumb villains | O06, O15, O28 |
| Private rewards | O02, O09, O19, O23 |
| Weak long-term engine | O15, O16, O24, O26 |
| Genre blend is messy | O04, O20, O29 |
| Regression is too solved | O07, O13, O30 |
| Support class loses identity | O14, O18, O29 |
| Episode endings are flat | O12, O21, O25, O27 |
