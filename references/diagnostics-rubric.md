# Diagnostics Rubric

Use this file when reviewing an idea, trope pack, title, synopsis, outline, episode plan, or project bible. Diagnose commercial function first, then suggest concrete repairs.

## Scorecard

Score each axis from 0 to 5.

| Axis | 0-1 Weak | 2-3 Usable | 4-5 Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genre recognition | Genre is hidden or confused. | Genre appears after setup. | Genre is clear immediately. |
| Reader promise | Mood only, no reward. | Reward exists but is broad. | Reward is specific and repeatable. |
| Protagonist advantage | Passive trait or backstory. | Advantage helps sometimes. | Advantage creates action and choices. |
| First proof | Explanation replaces proof. | Proof exists but lacks witness. | Scene visibly proves the concept. |
| Cliche use | Generic or copied-feeling. | Familiar but low variation. | Familiar reward with fresh scene path. |
| Successful cliche loop | Popular cliches are only listed. | Some common cliches work, but roles overlap. | Identity, advantage, proof, social reaction, renewal, and long-term hooks are all assigned. |
| Reward cadence | Too much setup or withholding. | Some rewards, uneven timing. | Small and large rewards are paced. |
| Institution reaction | World does not react. | A few characters react. | Institution, market, faction, or public reacts. |
| Social proof | Private gains only. | Occasional witness. | Recognition/reaction amplifies rewards. |
| Cost/consequence | Wins are free. | Costs appear late. | Rewards create new pressure. |
| Long-term engine | Only one arc idea. | Could run 25 episodes. | Can sustain 50+ episodes. |
| Freshness safety | New names only. | One useful twist. | Variation changes scenes, not just terms. |
| Originality safety | Too close to a known work. | Mostly generic but has risky echoes. | Uses broad genre grammar, no signature copying. |

## Approval Judgment

- **Pass**: average score 4+ and no critical axis below 3.
- **Revise**: average score 3+ but one or more weak axes can be repaired.
- **Rebuild**: unclear genre promise, passive protagonist, no first proof, or too-close copying risk.

Always give the judgment in plain Korean and explain what to change first.

## Critical Axes

If any of these fail, fix them before titles, prose, or detailed worldbuilding:

- genre recognition
- reader promise
- protagonist advantage
- first proof
- reward cadence
- originality safety

## Common Problems and Repairs

### The concept is only atmosphere.

- **Symptom**: setting mood is clear, but what readers receive is vague.
- **Repair**: name the reward channel and build a first proof scene.

### The protagonist is special but passive.

- **Symptom**: rare bloodline, hidden class, or regression exists, but the protagonist waits.
- **Repair**: give a decision that uses the advantage and creates consequence.

### The cliche is recognizable but flat.

- **Symptom**: rank test, hidden dungeon, boardroom revenge, or duel upset plays exactly as expected.
- **Repair**: keep the reward, change witness/proof object/institution/cost.

### The fresh idea breaks the genre.

- **Symptom**: the variation is clever but removes the expected pleasure.
- **Repair**: restore the primary genre reward first, then add novelty as pressure.

### The story cannot run long.

- **Symptom**: one strong opening but no ladder.
- **Repair**: build personal -> institutional -> regional -> national/world/system escalation.

### The story risks copying a specific work.

- **Symptom**: named institutions, signature rules, scene sequence, or character roles are too similar.
- **Repair**: abstract the function, change institution and proof object, rebuild scene order.

## Red Flags

Mark these explicitly:

- "The protagonist is described as smart but makes no smart choice."
- "The first three episodes are mostly explanation."
- "The only reward is a stat increase."
- "Everyone around the protagonist acts stupid to make the protagonist look good."
- "The secondary genre takes over before the primary promise is proven."
- "The twist is just a new term for an old device."
- "The pitch depends on knowledge of a famous reference work."

## Repair Priority

1. Clarify reader promise.
2. Move first proof earlier.
3. Make protagonist advantage actionable.
4. Add witness/social proof.
5. Add cost after reward.
6. Define repeatable reward loop.
7. Build 50-episode escalation.
8. Remove too-specific borrowed elements.
