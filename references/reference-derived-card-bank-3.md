# Reference-Derived Card Bank 3

Use this file after `reference-synthesis-engine-2.md` when the user wants new cards derived from diverse references rather than only genre defaults. These are abstract, safe-to-use cards, not copied scenes.

Each card includes: function, reward, proof, cost, and best conversion targets.

## Function Cards

### R3-001 Lack Becomes Asset

- **Function**: a missing quality is revealed as the reason ordinary measurement failed.
- **Reward**: recognition, status.
- **Proof**: rank category changes, exception granted, expert note.
- **Cost**: institution wants to control the new category.
- **Targets**: hunter, academy, murim, support class.

### R3-002 Forbidden Rule Has a Legal Owner

- **Function**: a taboo is not moral law but someone's property system.
- **Reward**: autonomy, knowledge.
- **Proof**: contract, sect seal, system clause, ownership registry.
- **Cost**: rule owner retaliates.
- **Targets**: tower, sect, chaebol, romance fantasy.

### R3-003 Donor Needs Testing Too

- **Function**: the helper tests the protagonist, but the protagonist also evaluates the helper.
- **Reward**: autonomy, relationship.
- **Proof**: rejected offer, renegotiated contract, conditional oath.
- **Cost**: aid is weaker at first but safer later.
- **Targets**: constellation, hidden master, investor, professor, contract marriage.

### R3-004 Aid Carries a Claim

- **Function**: magical or institutional aid is useful because it creates a future ownership dispute.
- **Reward**: power, money.
- **Proof**: title, manual, patent, deed, sponsor mark.
- **Cost**: previous owner, tax, taboo, or debt appears.
- **Targets**: tower, item market, murim, finance.

### R3-005 False Hero Has Real Infrastructure

- **Function**: the false hero is not merely fake; they control media, records, contracts, or institutional trust.
- **Reward**: revenge, justice.
- **Proof**: audit, replay, ledger, witness correction.
- **Cost**: exposing them destabilizes the system that depended on them.
- **Targets**: hunter, academy, sect, chaebol, rofan.

### R3-006 Return With Changed Access

- **Function**: the protagonist returns to a place that rejected them, but with a new access right.
- **Reward**: status, catharsis.
- **Proof**: license, invitation, token, board seat, rank.
- **Cost**: old enemies must obey publicly but sabotage privately.
- **Targets**: academy, sect, family, guild, company.

### R3-007 Pursuit as Audit

- **Function**: chase pressure is converted into inspection, investigation, license review, or debt collection.
- **Reward**: tension, justice.
- **Proof**: summons, audit notice, guild inquiry, school hearing.
- **Cost**: protagonist must prove value under hostile procedure.
- **Targets**: hunter, finance, academy, revenge.

### R3-008 Impossible Task With Hidden Audience

- **Function**: an impossible task is actually a staged observation.
- **Reward**: recognition, status.
- **Proof**: hidden sponsor/elder/professor/investor reaction.
- **Cost**: being noticed creates recruitment pressure.
- **Targets**: tower, academy, murim, chaebol.

### R3-009 Recognition Token Is a Liability

- **Function**: the thing that proves status also marks the protagonist as responsible.
- **Reward**: status, authority.
- **Proof**: token, title, license, seal, heirloom.
- **Cost**: obligations, enemies, taxes, or inherited debts attach.
- **Targets**: murim, tower, guild, family, finance.

### R3-010 Rescue Creates Political Debt

- **Function**: deliverance saves someone but changes faction balance.
- **Reward**: safety, relationship.
- **Proof**: rescued witness, public gratitude, changed vote, faction move.
- **Cost**: rescued person's enemies become protagonist's enemies.
- **Targets**: hunter, rofan, academy, murim.

## Motif Cards

### R3-011 Door That Opens Backward

- **Function**: forbidden access is earned by leaving, refusing, or protecting instead of entering.
- **Reward**: knowledge, autonomy.
- **Proof**: sealed floor, archive, lab, cave, family room opens.
- **Cost**: watchers know the protagonist met a hidden criterion.
- **Targets**: tower, possession, murim, academy.

### R3-012 Contract That Rewards Refusal

- **Function**: the best reward comes from not accepting the obvious deal.
- **Reward**: autonomy, intelligence.
- **Proof**: better hidden clause, sponsor reaction, board memo.
- **Cost**: short-term resources are lost.
- **Targets**: constellation, chaebol, rofan, guild.

### R3-013 Prophecy as Market Signal

- **Function**: prophecy is useful because it changes prices, behavior, or supply.
- **Reward**: money, knowledge.
- **Proof**: market movement, bidding war, land price, item scarcity.
- **Cost**: speculation creates public suspicion.
- **Targets**: regression, finance, hunter, tower.

### R3-014 Token With Two Audiences

- **Function**: the same symbol means status to one group and threat to another.
- **Reward**: status, tension.
- **Proof**: rank/title/seal interpreted differently.
- **Cost**: protagonist must manage split reputation.
- **Targets**: murim, academy, rofan, tower.

### R3-015 Hidden Helper Is the Future Victim

- **Function**: donor/mentor matters because they are doomed later.
- **Reward**: relationship, safety.
- **Proof**: small clue that connects aid to future death.
- **Cost**: helping early changes the mentor's behavior.
- **Targets**: regression, possession, academy, murim.

### R3-016 False Trail Is Useful

- **Function**: the obvious wrong clue is wrong about the enemy but right about the system.
- **Reward**: knowledge, mystery.
- **Proof**: misleading evidence reveals real metric or owner.
- **Cost**: protagonist must admit partial error publicly.
- **Targets**: mystery, revenge, tower, finance.

### R3-017 Object Becomes Venue

- **Function**: an item is not just a reward; it opens a place of judgment.
- **Reward**: discovery, status.
- **Proof**: deed opens board seat, manual opens trial, title opens floor, ring opens court.
- **Cost**: access triggers procedure.
- **Targets**: finance, murim, tower, rofan.

### R3-018 Scar as Receipt

- **Function**: injury or stigma becomes proof of service, ownership, or survival.
- **Reward**: recognition, justice.
- **Proof**: scar, curse mark, penalty trace, contract mark.
- **Cost**: mark also identifies the protagonist to enemies.
- **Targets**: hunter, tower, murim, romance fantasy.

### R3-019 Ledger of Debts

- **Function**: relationship, revenge, or rescue is tracked as debts owed and repaid.
- **Reward**: justice, relationship.
- **Proof**: ledger, oath, rescue record, favor list.
- **Cost**: debt can be transferred or inherited.
- **Targets**: murim, chaebol, rofan, guild.

### R3-020 Countdown With Wrong Unit

- **Function**: timer is real but measured in reputation, trust, debt, rank, or casualties rather than time.
- **Reward**: tension, competence.
- **Proof**: meter, public score, market index, system gauge.
- **Cost**: protagonist must decide what to sacrifice to slow it.
- **Targets**: system, academy, finance, support class.

## Graph Cards

### R3-021 Reward Creates Witness

- **Function**: payoff itself summons the person who can verify or challenge it.
- **Reward**: social proof.
- **Proof**: bid, title, rank, item, apology triggers witness.
- **Cost**: witness has agenda.
- **Targets**: all.

### R3-022 Witness Creates Institution

- **Function**: a witness does not just react; they cause a formal institution to respond.
- **Reward**: scale.
- **Proof**: report, hearing, ranking review, board meeting.
- **Cost**: public procedure begins.
- **Targets**: hunter, academy, finance, rofan.

### R3-023 Cost Creates Antagonist

- **Function**: antagonist appears because the protagonist's win damaged a system.
- **Reward**: coherent escalation.
- **Proof**: enemy loses revenue, authority, reputation, or secrecy.
- **Cost**: enemy is structurally motivated.
- **Targets**: all.

### R3-024 Antagonist Mirrors Advantage

- **Function**: the villain uses the same cliche more ruthlessly.
- **Reward**: suspense.
- **Proof**: second system, second regressor, copied skill, hostile investor, rival prophecy.
- **Cost**: protagonist must evolve method.
- **Targets**: regression, system, finance, academy.

### R3-025 Hook Object Changes Owner

- **Function**: the episode hook is ownership transfer.
- **Reward**: next-click pressure.
- **Proof**: claim notice, deed, token, title, contract assignment.
- **Cost**: protagonist has to defend a reward they just earned.
- **Targets**: all item/contract genres.

### R3-026 Small Payoff Unlocks Big Question

- **Function**: pay trust before mystery.
- **Reward**: satisfaction plus curiosity.
- **Proof**: concrete win, then name/date/object appears.
- **Cost**: bigger system notices.
- **Targets**: all serials.

### R3-027 Route Deviation Becomes New Route

- **Function**: avoiding the script does not create freedom; it creates a different structured path.
- **Reward**: autonomy, uncertainty.
- **Proof**: new invitation, new quest, new favorability, new enemy.
- **Cost**: old knowledge loses precision.
- **Targets**: possession, regression, rofan, game novel.

### R3-028 Institution Measures the Wrong Virtue

- **Function**: institution is rational but optimizes the wrong value.
- **Reward**: intelligence, justice.
- **Proof**: protagonist's alternate metric predicts better results.
- **Cost**: changing the metric threatens vested interests.
- **Targets**: hunter, academy, finance, sect.

### R3-029 Twist Reclassifies Genre

- **Function**: a familiar scene is revealed to belong to another genre engine.
- **Reward**: freshness.
- **Proof**: duel is contract, auction is trial, date is negotiation, exam is rescue.
- **Cost**: both genre institutions now react.
- **Targets**: hybrid concepts.

### R3-030 Ending Hook Is a Procedure

- **Function**: instead of a random threat, the episode ends with a formal process.
- **Reward**: concrete next click.
- **Proof**: summons, audit, duel notice, vote, hearing, invitation, bid deadline.
- **Cost**: protagonist cannot ignore it without losing access.
- **Targets**: all commercial serials.

## Use Rule

For a quick upgrade, select:

- 2 function cards;
- 2 motif cards;
- 2 graph cards;
- 1 remix operator from `cliche-remix-operators.md`;
- 1 first proof scene from `scene-proof-bank.md`.

For a full concept, connect 8-12 cards into a graph where every card causes the next.
